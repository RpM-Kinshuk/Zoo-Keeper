# D6: Freeze the test plan separately from the conclusions?
Status: open | Class: 3 | Ask: approve option A
Due: Nov 14 review | If no answer: keep the current rule; later tests use separate written protocols and their unverified claims stay outside the frozen paper claim list.

Recommendation: A (confidence: high).
Why: We currently freeze supported paper claims on Nov 25 and the evaluation plan on Dec 4. But real-model checks finish Dec 1–5 and the large-search test Dec 10. A test plan must be able to ask questions whose answers are still unknown.

Options:
- A: The Dec 4 document separates approved, verified claims from open questions, with tests and pass rules fixed before their final runs. Gain: honest advance planning for later evidence. Lose: some paper conclusions remain unsettled until later review.
- B: Keep the current document restricted to Nov 25 approved claims. Gain: simplest early freeze. Lose: later hypothesis tests remain separate and the paper scope may be unnecessarily narrow.

Big-picture effect:
- Real-world use: real published models still have to pass; no shortcut from lab success.
- Claims: an unanswered question is never an established result. Nov 25 still solidifies only already-verified claims; later claim changes require the overseer's explicit review.
- Papers and dates: all existing dates and the Dec 18 venue decision stay unchanged; this adjusts what the Dec 4 document may contain.
- Simplicity and cost: one test-plan document with two clearly labeled sections; no new compute or spending.
- Safety and risk: no release or access change. Do not retune tests after inspecting final outcomes or call already-seen data an unseen test.

Can it be undone? Yes, by removing the open-question section; preserve any test rules already used to interpret a result.
Uncertainty: some proposed questions may not have enough independent test cases or usable real-model truth; this decision does not supply them.
Second opinion (fresh skeptic, Nov 8): agree. Separate open questions from approved conclusions; freeze tests before final data inspection, label already-seen cases as development evidence, and require overseer approval before later results become paper claims.
